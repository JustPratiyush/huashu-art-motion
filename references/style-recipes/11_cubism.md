# 11_cubism · 1912 Analytic Cubism (Picasso / Braque)

File: `scenes/11_cubism.js` (about 280 lines). Performance: warm mean 13ms, max 19ms, first frame (building caches) ≈100ms; determinism passes; lt 0.34→0.46 motion diff 6.9% of pixels. On screen for 0.47s in the film (T10 shard transition 0.24s).

## Pipeline
1. **Background fully cached `cb_bg` — "transparent stacked planes"**: core function `plane(g, pts, col, alpha, r)`: each plane gets one linear gradient, starting at vertex 0 and ending at another randomly chosen vertex, with three color stops = 25% mixed with cream → the base color → 40% mixed with dark brown, alpha 0.18–0.7. Stacking: 18 large hand-placed planes (positioned per the original's composition) + 9 radiating fan planes under the table (from (1000,1090), alternating light and dark at α0.3) + 70 random 3/4-sided polygons (avoiding the year area at top right). Many layers of semi-transparent gradients stacked together produce the analytic-cubist space that is "transparent, interpenetrating, every plane one light side and one dark side".
   - Texture planes: 4 stippled planes (9px diamond grid r1.6), 4 hatched planes (diagonal lines at 7px spacing), a sheet of lined paper along the left edge.
   - Braque's short-dash sand texture: 9000 small 3–8×1.5px dashes, 55% cream at α0.28 / 45% dark brown at α0.16 — the original's "brick" texture depends on this.
   - 46 thin straight lines running through (1.3px α0.55) = passage.
   - Faux wood grain `wood()`: S/6 wavy lines along the long side, with a Gaussian bump `14·exp(-(u-k)²/0.01)` at knot positions, knots as 4 rings of ellipses. Used for the tabletop and the board on the right (Braque's comb-painted faux wood). Cane chair back = 10px diamond-hole dots + crosshatched diagonals.
2. **Window panes sliding as shards**: the window view is cached separately as a large 560×540 image (55 blue-gray facets + 3 diagonal light beams + the Eiffel Tower + seagull arcs); each of the four panes clips it and frames it at a different offset `dx = sin(9lt+1.7i)·18 + 10fps jitter ±3`, `dy = cos(7lt+i)·8` — the same view misaligned across four panes = multiple viewpoints assembled; each pane also has a semi-transparent triangle sliding across at 260px/s.
3. **CAFÉ stencil letters jumping and swapping**: `AlfaSlabOne-400` 104px drawn into a small offscreen layer, stepping at 12fps (`st=floor(lt·12)`), cycling through `CAFÉ/ACFÉ/CAÉF/CFAÉ`; each step, each letter gets y ±13 and rotation ±0.11rad (from a hash); then `destination-out` cuts horizontal and vertical gaps = stencil letters (the gaps reveal the planes beneath, more convincing than painting paper-colored strips).
4. **Newspaper JOU**: a cached 300×190 image (LibreBaskerville-700 84px "JOU" + 12px headline "LA BATAILLE / S'EST ENGAGÉ UN CHAT" + three columns of gray placeholder text), trembling at 8fps each frame (rotation ±0.0125rad, x ±1.5px), the top-right dog-ear flipping with `sin(20lt)`.
5. **Characters = a custom facet cutter `facetize`**: RIG flat fill (fill mode, no lines) into an offscreen layer → read back the pixels of the bounding box → jittered triangle mesh (each cell picks a random diagonal) → each triangle samples color at its centroid, light/dark `×(0.8+0.38·rand)`, plus a light→dark gradient from vertex 0 to vertex 2 → **triangles are not clipped** and are drawn straight back (skip those whose centroid alpha<120). Not clipping is the key: the silhouette edge is cut into jagged straight edges, and some triangles poke out beyond the outline — exactly the cubist feel of "planes spilling past the contour".
   - Girl cell 58 (large planes); the hair area gets a second, finer pass at cell 26 that only cuts "colors that look like blonde hair" (`only: R>150 && B<130 && R-B>60`); cat cell 26.
   - Vertices jitter ±8% of a cell at 8fps, and 16–35% of triangles jump ±12% in brightness (boil) → the characters "quiver", which is exactly what the cat area does in the original at frames 548–562.
6. **After cutting, add Picasso-style structure**: the face gets two skin-tone light/dark planes laid again (the facets drag blonde color into the face) + a blue-gray plane over the front half of the face at α0.82 + a frontal almond eye (a frontal eye on a profile face) + spiral ear + red lips; the white collar as two light/dark triangles + three buttons; the skirt with 8 radiating cut lines from the waist + alternating light/dark wedges; outer contours as straight segments `segs()`, each extending 10–16px beyond both ends (lines drawn past the shape). Cat: hexagonal straight-line head outline, `bodyPts` straight-line body outline, yellow diamond eyes + slit pupils, triangular nose in profile.
7. On the very top, 5 planes at α0.2 lie across the person and cat and shift by `sin(6lt)·8` — "embedding" the characters in the space so they don't look pasted onto a background.

## Pitfalls (important)
- **Nondeterminism: the first frame differs from the rest.** Two stacked causes, both only on the first frame: ① an offscreen layer that will be read with `getImageData` is drawn on the GPU the first time; after readback Chrome converts it to CPU rasterization with different anti-aliasing → different centroid colors → different triangle colors; ② more importantly: the offscreen layer's context state (`lineJoin='round'` set in facetize, `lineCap='round'` in segs) carries over to the next frame, while the first frame uses the default miter/butt. Fix: create your own layer with `getContext('2d',{willReadFrequently:true})`, and **at the start of every frame use `g.reset()` (clears pixels + state) rather than `clearRect`**. It was only found by comparing hashes of "the same lt rendered three times in a row".
- At facet cell 34 the whole body was covered in small triangles, like a low-poly 3D model rather than cubism; only at 58 do you get "large light/dark planes".
- The second, finer hair pass at first used a circular clip, which turned half the face gold too — hence the color filter `only`.

## Still room to improve
- The girl's overall outline is still RIG's realistic curve; the facets only change the edges. A more thorough approach would replace shoulders, elbows and knees with a polyline skeleton before cutting.
- The original is darker overall with stronger light/dark contrast (more large dark-brown planes); this version is too bright.
