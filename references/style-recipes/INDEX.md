# Master Style Index (35 styles, updated 2026-10-05)

> **Read this sentence first: all 35 sample films share one composition** (window at top left, cat at bottom left, girl on the right holding a cup; `assets/all-styles-overview.jpg` is the same). That is the mechanism of the Art History Speedrun film — the style changes while the scene stays the same — not the composition of these styles. For a new topic, compose afresh for that topic: you can take the renderer, brushwork, color logic and motif motion from a card, but do not carry over the room's coordinates, characters or furniture.

To choose a style, look at the two tables below first, then open the matching card. "Quality" is the current level of what has actually been built (★★★ recognizable at a glance, ready to use; ★★ recognizable but with clear weaknesses; ★ needs to be redone), not the difficulty of the style itself. The weakness is exactly what to beat first the next time you build that style.

Styles come in two layers. **Material layer**: handling of brushwork, materials, light and color that can be laid over any topic (products, data, stories all work). **Era specimens**: tightly bound to a particular era or artist; suited to speedruns, time-travel and tribute films, and they usually look out of place when used for other topics. If unsure, put it in the material layer; when a style touches both (e.g., Hopper's hard-edged patches of light are a transferable lighting treatment), judge by how you use it.

## Quality distribution and next steps

- ★★★ 20 styles, ★★ 14 styles, ★ 1 style.
- **The most common weakness is characters**: once the background goes through the style renderer it looks like that style, but a character made only from a flat-colored vector rig looks pasted on (proven practice #8). The next round of quality work starts with "characters go through the same renderer / are reshaped in that style's forms": Rembrandt, Picasso blue, ink wash, Ghibli, Dalí, Marvel.
